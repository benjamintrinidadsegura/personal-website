## Three directions and critical comparison

A - Cinematic Blue: one large cool-toned Spotlight portrait, broad negative space and large sans-serif typography. The lens overlaps the image field to create depth; an understated horizontal destination index becomes compact editorial rows on mobile. This has the strongest human/film-poster emphasis. Its weakness is the available source portrait resolution and reliance on one Spotlight subject.

B - Liquid Glass Editorial: a bounded portrait aperture on the left and an attached light material field on the right. Serif headline, dark typography, a more luminous lens and a vertical destination index form an editorial spread. On mobile the material/reading column remains on the right rather than stacking the desktop. This has the clearest contrast from the older dark Labs. The field can feel more architectural than liquid; that is a founder-review question, not proof of exceptional material craftsmanship.

C - Spatial Discovery: four actual content paths with different silhouettes: portrait aperture, atlas, tool illustration and freestanding Writing typography. Quiet paths originate at the search representation; they do not assert fictional relationships between people/projects. Mobile uses a small asymmetric composition around the lens. It is the most directly exploratory, but closest to the previous universe metaphor and still mixes photographic and illustrative source genres.
